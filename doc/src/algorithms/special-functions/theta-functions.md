In genus $g$, for $\tau$ in the Siegel
upper half-space $\mathcal{H}_g$ (symmetric complex matrices with
positive definite imaginary part $Y$), $z \in \mathbb{C}^g$ and characteristics
$a, b \in \{0, 1\}^g$,

$$
\theta_{a,b}(z,\tau) = \sum_{n\in \Z^{g} + \tfrac a2} \exp\bigl(\pi i n^T \tau n + 2\pi i n^T (z + \tfrac b2)\bigr).
$$

For $g = 1$ these are the four Jacobi theta functions:
$\theta_{0,0} = \theta_3$, $\theta_{0,1} = \theta_4$, $\theta_{1,0} = \theta_2$
and $\theta_{1,1} = -\theta_1$. FLINT evaluates them in two steps: an
action of the modular group moves $(z, \tau)$ to a *reduced* point, where
the series converges quickly and uniformly, and the theta values at the
reduced point are computed either by summing the series (described
below) or by the quasi-linear algorithm based on duplication formulas
([](algorithms/special-functions/theta-quasi-linear)). The general
references are [@Mum1983] and [@Igu1972]; the algorithms of
[`acb_theta`](acb_theta) are those of Elkies and Kieffer [@EK2025].

## Size of the terms {#size}

Writing $y = \operatorname{Im}(z)$ and $\lVert x \rVert_\tau^2 = \pi x^T Y x$,
the term of index $n$ has absolute value

$$
\bigl| e^{\pi i n^T \tau n + 2\pi i n^T (z + b/2)} \bigr|
= e^{\pi y^T Y^{-1} y} \, e^{-\lVert n + Y^{-1} y \rVert_\tau^2}.
$$

Hence the terms decrease like a Gaussian centered at $v = -Y^{-1}y$,
$|\theta_{a,b}(z,\tau)| \lesssim e^{\pi y^T Y^{-1} y}$, and, barring
cancellation, $|\theta_{a,b}(z,\tau)| \approx e^{\pi y^T Y^{-1} y} e^{-d^2}$
where $d$ is the distance from $v$ to $\Z^g + \frac a2$. The normalised
function $\widetilde\theta_{a,b} = e^{-\pi y^T Y^{-1} y}\theta_{a,b}$ is
bounded, and FLINT measures precision in terms of its absolute error
([](acb_theta/conventions#precision)).

## Reduction {#reduction}

The symplectic group $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\Z)$ acts on $\mathbb{C}^g \times \mathcal{H}_g$
by $m \cdot (z, \tau) = ((\gamma\tau + \delta)^{-T} z, (\alpha\tau + \beta)(\gamma\tau + \delta)^{-1})$,
and the theta transformation formula [@Igu1972, p. 176]

$$
\theta_{a,b}(m\cdot(z,\tau)) = \kappa(m) \zeta_8^{e(m, a, b)} \det(\gamma\tau + \delta)^{1/2} e^{\pi i z^T (\gamma\tau + \delta)^{-1} \gamma z} \theta_{a',b'}(z,\tau)
$$

relates the theta values at $(z, \tau)$ and at $m \cdot (z, \tau)$, with an
explicit permutation of characteristics, an 8th root of unity, and a
square root of $\det(\gamma\tau + \delta)$ whose branch must be tracked
(`acb_siegel_kappa`). The group is generated by the block-diagonal
matrices $\operatorname{diag}(U, U^{-T})$ (a change of basis of the
lattice), the trigonal matrices $\begin{pmatrix} I & S \\ 0 & I \end{pmatrix}$
(integer translations of $\tau$), and $J$.

A matrix $\tau$ is *reduced* (Siegel's fundamental domain, in a relaxed
form) if

1. $Y$ is reduced (FLINT uses LLL reduction with parameters
   $(0.99, 0.51)$, as a practical substitute for Minkowski reduction);
2. $|\operatorname{Re}(\tau_{j,k})| \le \frac12$ for all $j, k$;
3. $|\det(\gamma\tau + \delta)| \ge 1$ for a finite set of
   *fundamental* symplectic matrices.

For $g = 1$ this is the usual fundamental domain
$|\operatorname{Re}\tau| \le \frac12$, $|\tau| \ge 1$, where the only
fundamental matrix is $J$. For $g = 2$, Gottschling [@Got1959] found the
19 matrices that define the boundary of the fundamental domain. For
$g \ge 3$ no such list is known, and FLINT uses the $19 g(g-1)/2$
matrices obtained by embedding the genus 2 matrices on pairs of
coordinates and the $2^g$ embeddings of $J$ on subsets of coordinates
(`sp2gz_fundamental`). `acb_siegel_reduce` alternates the three
reductions until no fundamental matrix increases $\det Y$; in exact
arithmetic, $\det Y$ increases strictly with each such step and is
bounded on an orbit, so the iteration terminates. For reduced $\tau$, the diagonal
entries of the Cholesky factor of $\pi Y$ are bounded below by a constant
depending only on $g$ (for $g = 1$, $\pi \operatorname{Im}\tau \ge \pi\sqrt3/2$).

The vector $z$ is then reduced by the quasi-periodicity in the lattice
$\Z^g + \tau\Z^g$: subtracting $\tau r + s$ with $r, s \in 2\Z^g$ multiplies
every $\theta_{a,b}$ by an explicit exponential, and choosing $r$ close
to $Y^{-1}y$ and $s$ close to $\operatorname{Re}(z - \tau r)$ makes
$Y^{-1}y$ and $\operatorname{Re}(z)$ bounded (`acb_theta_reduce_z`).

## Summation over ellipsoids {#summation}

After reduction, $\widetilde\theta_{a,b}(z, \tau)$ is approximated by the
sum over the lattice points $n \in \Z^g + \frac a2$ with
$\lVert n - v \rVert_\tau \le R$, an ellipsoid; the shift by $a/2$ is
handled by
$\theta_{a,b}(z,\tau) = e^{\pi i a^T\tau a/4 + \pi i a^T(z + b/2)} \theta_{0,b}(z + \tau\tfrac{a}{2},\tau)$.
Write $\pi Y = C^T C$ with $C$ upper triangular with positive diagonal
entries $\gamma_0, \ldots, \gamma_{g-1}$.

::: theorem Tail bound [@EK2025] {#thm-tail}
For every $v \in \mathbb{R}^g$ and $R \ge 2$,

$$
\sum_{\substack{n \in \Z^g \\ \lVert C(n + v) \rVert \ge R}} e^{-\lVert C(n + v)\rVert^2}
\le \Bigl(1 + \sqrt{\tfrac{8}{\pi}}\Bigr) R^{g-1} e^{-R^2} \prod_{j=0}^{g-1} \Bigl(1 + \frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\gamma_j}\Bigr).
$$
:::

The factors $1 + \sqrt{2\pi}/\gamma_j$ account for the spacing
$\gamma_j$ of the lattice points along the successive coordinates of the
triangular basis, and $R^{g-1}e^{-R^2}$ is the order of the Gaussian
integral over the outside of a ball of radius $R$; see [@EK2025] for the
proof. Weighted versions with $\lVert \cdot \rVert^{\mathit{ord}}$
bound the tails of the differentiated series.
`acb_theta_sum_radius` solves
$(1 + \sqrt{8/\pi}) R^{g - 1 + 2\,\mathit{ord}} e^{-R^2} \prod_j (1 + \sqrt{2\pi}/\gamma_j) \le 2^{-p}$
for $R^2$, so that $R^2 \approx p \log 2 + O(g \log p)$, and
`acb_theta_sum_jet_radius` adapts the radius to derivatives.

**Ellipsoids.** The lattice points with
$\lVert C n + C v \rVert \le R$ are enumerated recursively on the last
coordinate: for fixed $n_{d}, \ldots, n_{g-1}$ the allowed values of
$n_{d-1}$ form an interval, computed from the triangular structure of
$C$, and each value has a child ellipsoid of dimension $d - 1$
(`acb_theta_eld_set`). This uses $O(R^{g-1})$ memory for $O(R^g)$ points,
an approach also used by [@DHBHS2004]. When several vectors $z$ differ
by small amounts, one ellipsoid with a union of centers serves all of
them.

**Summation.** The terms are summed line by line: on a line with fixed
$n_1, \ldots, n_{g-1}$, the terms are
$c \cdot e^{\pi i j^2 \tau_{0,0}} x^j$ for $n_{\min} \le j \le n_{\max}$,
where $x$ and the cofactor $c$ depend on the fixed coordinates and are
obtained from those of neighbouring lines by a few multiplications. The
powers $e^{\pi i j^2 \tau_{0,0}}$ are computed once for the ellipsoid,
the powers $x^j$ by repeated multiplication, and each line is summed
with one dot product (`acb_dot`), so that fewer than two complex
multiplications are needed per lattice point on average. Terms far
from the center are small and are computed at a lower precision. With
$R^2 \approx p\log 2$ and $\gamma_j$ bounded below, the ellipsoid
contains $O(p^{g/2})$ points, so summation costs $O(p^{g/2} M(p))$ for
fixed $g$; it is the method of choice at low precision and the base case
of the quasi-linear algorithm, where it is applied at $2^m\tau$ with
$O(1)$ points.

**Derivatives.** Differentiating the series multiplies the term of index
$n$ by $(2\pi i)^{|k|} n_0^{k_0} \cdots n_{g-1}^{k_{g-1}}$; the summation
workers accumulate these products (`acb_theta_sum_jet`), and the tail
bound includes the polynomial weight.

## Genus 1 {#genus-one}

For $g = 1$, `acb_modular_theta_sum` sums the four Jacobi series
together. With $q = e^{\pi i \tau}$ and $w = e^{\pi i z}$,

$$
\theta_3(z,\tau) = 1 + \sum_{n \ge 1} q^{n^2} (w^{2n} + w^{-2n}),
\qquad
\frac{\theta_2(z,\tau)}{q_{1/4}} = \sum_{n \ge 0} q^{n(n+1)} (w^{2n+1} + w^{-2n-1}),
$$

and similarly for $\theta_4$ and $\theta_1$ with alternating signs. The
exponents of $q$, interleaved, are $\lfloor (k+2)^2/4 \rfloor$,
$k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$, that is $1, 2, 4, 6, 9, 12, \ldots$, and the
exponents of $w$ are $\pm(k+2)$. The powers of $q$ are computed with a
short addition sequence for this set of exponents [@EHJ2016], in which
most powers cost one multiplication, and only even powers of $w$ and
$w^{-1}$ are needed when the odd terms are written as
$w (w^{2n} \pm w^{-2n-2})$.

::: lemma Truncation bound for genus 1 {#lem-genus-one}
Let $Q = |q| < 1$, $W = \max(|w|, |w|^{-1})$, and stop the summation
before the term of index $k = N$. With $E = \lfloor (N+2)^2/4 \rfloor$
and $F = \lfloor (N+1)/2 \rfloor + 1$, the error of each of the four
series, and of its $r$-th derivative with respect to $\pi z$, is at most

$$
\frac{2 Q^E W^{N+2} (N+2)^r}{1 - Q^F W e^{r/(N+2)}},
$$

provided that the denominator is positive.
:::

::: proof
The term of index $k$ (in whichever series it belongs to) is bounded by
$2 Q^{E_k} W^{k+2}$ with $E_k = \lfloor (k+2)^2/4 \rfloor$, where the
factor 2 accounts for $w^{\pm(k+2)}$, and the omitted terms of each
series are among those with $k \ge N$. Since
$E_{k+1} - E_k = \lfloor (k+3)/2 \rfloor \ge F$ for $k \ge N$, the
bounds of consecutive terms decrease at least by the ratio $Q^F W$.
Differentiating $r$ times with respect to $\pi z$ multiplies the term of
index $k$ by at most $(k+2)^r$, and
$((N+2+m)/(N+2))^r \le e^{mr/(N+2)}$. Summing the geometric series gives
the bound.
:::

`acb_modular_theta_sum` chooses the smallest $N$ with
$Q^{E} W^{N+2} < 2^{-p}$, which is $N \approx 2\sqrt{p/\log_2(1/Q)}$;
after reduction (to $|\tau|^2 \ge 63/64$), $Q < 0.07$, so
$N = O(\sqrt{p})$, and $z$ has been reduced so that $W$ is bounded.
`acb_theta_sum` calls it for $g = 1$, and the theta constants alone
($z = 0$) use the specialised `acb_modular_theta_const_sum`, with
rectangular splitting for many terms.
