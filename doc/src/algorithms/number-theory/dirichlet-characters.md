A Dirichlet character modulo $q$ is a group homomorphism $\chi :
(\Z/q\Z)^\times \to \C^\times$, extended to $\Z$ by periodicity and by
$\chi(n) = 0$ for $\gcd(n, q) > 1$. For the theory see [@Apo1976, Chapter 6] and
[@IR1990, Chapter 4]; the numbering is that of Conrey, used by the LMFDB
[@LMFDB].

## The unit group {#group}

By the Chinese remainder theorem $(\Z/q\Z)^\times \cong \prod_{p^e \| q}
(\Z/p^e\Z)^\times$, and the factors have the following structure
[@IR1990, Theorem 2 of Chapter 4].

::: theorem {#thm-structure}
For an odd prime $p$, $(\Z/p^e\Z)^\times$ is cyclic of order $p^{e-1}(p
- 1)$, and an integer $g$ generates it for every $e \ge 1$ if and only
if $g$ generates $(\Z/p^2\Z)^\times$. For $e \ge 3$, $(\Z/2^e\Z)^\times$
is the direct product of the subgroup $\{\pm 1\}$ and the cyclic subgroup
of order $2^{e-2}$ generated by 5; $(\Z/4\Z)^\times = \{\pm 1\}$ and
$(\Z/2\Z)^\times$ is trivial.
:::

The *Conrey generators* are: for odd $p$, the smallest positive integer
that is a primitive root modulo $p^2$; for $8 \mid q$, the two
generators $-1$ and $5$ of the 2-part; for $4 \| q$, the generator $-1$.
For all primes up to $10^{16}$ except $p = 40487$ and $p = 6692367337$,
the smallest primitive root modulo $p$ is also a primitive root modulo
$p^2$, so FLINT computes it with `n_primitive_root_prime` and treats the
two exceptions separately (the generators are 10 and 7); larger prime
factors are not supported. Each generator $g_k$ of order $\varphi_k$ is
lifted to $\tilde g_k \equiv g_k \pmod{p^e}$, $\tilde g_k \equiv 1
\pmod{q/p^e}$, so that every $m \in (\Z/q\Z)^\times$ is uniquely

$$
m = \prod_k \tilde g_k^{\,a_k}, \qquad 0 \le a_k < \varphi_k.
$$

The vector $(a_k)$ is the *log* of $m$. The exponent of the group is $e
= \operatorname{lcm}_k \varphi_k$.

## Characters and the Conrey pairing {#pairing}

A character is determined by its values on the generators, which are
$\varphi_k$-th roots of unity. Conrey's choice is

$$
\chi_q(m, n) = \exp\left(2 \pi i \sum_k \frac{a_k b_k}{\varphi_k}\right)
$$

for $m, n$ with logs $(a_k)$ and $(b_k)$. This is a nondegenerate
symmetric bilinear pairing on $(\Z/q\Z)^\times$, so $m \mapsto \chi_q(m,
\cdot)$ is an isomorphism from the group onto its dual. As a
consequence, the character group operations are those of the numbers
($\chi_q(m, \cdot)\chi_q(m', \cdot) = \chi_q(mm', \cdot)$), the order of
$\chi_q(m, \cdot)$ is the multiplicative order of $m$, and by symmetry
$\chi_q(m, -1) = \chi_q(-1, m)$. Values are exact roots of unity
$\zeta_e^v$ with $v = \sum_k a_k b_k (e/\varphi_k) \bmod e$, which the
module returns as the exponent $v$.

## Conductor, parity and primitivity {#properties}

A character modulo $q$ is *induced* by a character modulo $f \mid q$ if
it is the composition with the reduction $(\Z/q\Z)^\times \to
(\Z/f\Z)^\times$. The *conductor* is the smallest such $f$, and $\chi$ is
*primitive* if its conductor is $q$.

::: proposition {#prop-conductor}
The conductor of $\chi_q(m, \cdot)$ is the product over the components
of the following factors. For an odd $p^e \| q$ with log entry $a$: 1 if
$a = 0$, and $p^{e - v_p(a)}$ otherwise. For the 2-part: 1 if both log
entries are zero; 4 if only the entry of $-1$ is nonzero; $2^{e -
v_2(a)}$ if the entry $a$ of $5$ is nonzero.
:::

::: proof
The character is induced modulo $f$ if and only if it is trivial on the
kernel of the reduction modulo $f$, which is a product over the
components. For a cyclic component generated by $g$ of order $\varphi =
\varphi(p^e)$, the kernel of the reduction to $p^f$ is generated by
$g^{\varphi(p^f)}$, and $\chi(g^{\varphi(p^f)}) = \exp(2\pi i\, a
\varphi(p^f)/\varphi(p^e))$ is 1 if and only if $p^{e-f} \mid a$. The
smallest such $f$ is $e - v_p(a)$. The 2-part is analogous, with the
kernel of the reduction from $2^e$ to $2^f$, $f \ge 3$, generated by
$5^{2^{f-2}}$, and to $4$ by $5$.
:::

From the number $m$ instead of the log, the $p$-part of the conductor is
$p^{1 + t}$, where $t \ge 0$ is the least integer with $m^{(p-1)p^t}
\equiv 1 \pmod{p^e}$ (and 1 if $m \equiv 1 \pmod {p^e}$), since the
order of $m$ in the component determines $v_p(a)$. The parity follows
from the symmetry of the pairing:

::: proposition {#prop-parity}
$\chi_q(m, -1) = \epsilon \cdot \left(\frac{m}{q'}\right)$, where $q'$ is
the product of the distinct odd primes dividing $q$, $(\cdot/\cdot)$ is
the Jacobi symbol, and $\epsilon = -1$ if $4 \mid q$ and $m \equiv 3
\pmod 4$, otherwise $\epsilon = 1$.
:::

::: proof
$\chi_q(m, -1) = \chi_q(-1, m)$. In an odd component, $-1 = g^{\varphi/2}$
has log entry $\varphi/2$, contributing $\exp(\pi i b) = (-1)^b$ where
$b$ is the log entry of $m$; $b$ is even exactly when $m$ is a square
modulo $p$, so the contribution is the Legendre symbol $(m/p)$. In the
2-part, $-1$ has entry 1 on the generator $-1$ and 0 on $5$, contributing
$(-1)^{a_0}$ with $a_0 = 1$ exactly when $m \equiv 3 \pmod 4$.
:::

Counting the logs that give conductor $q$ in each component, the number
of primitive characters is multiplicative: $p - 2$ for $p \| q$, $(p -
1)^2 p^{e-2}$ for $p^e \| q$ with $e \ge 2$, and for the 2-part 0, 1 and
$2^{e-2}$ for $2 \| q$, $4 \| q$ and $2^e \| q$ with $e \ge 3$.

## Computing values {#values}

The value $\chi_q(m, n)$ requires the log of $n$, hence a discrete
logarithm in each odd component; the 2-part needs only $n \bmod 4$ and a
2-adic logarithm. These are computed with the `dlog` module
([](algorithms/number-theory/discrete-log-tables)): a single logarithm
with `dlog_once`, or with tables precomputed for many calls.

For the vector of values $\chi(0), \ldots, \chi(N-1)$ there are two
strategies.

- **Loop over the group.** Enumerating the logs in lexicographic order
  (`dirichlet_char_next`), each step multiplies the number by one
  generator and adds a constant to the value exponent, so all $\varphi(q)$
  values cost $O(\varphi(q))$ operations without any discrete logarithm.
  This is best when $N$ is comparable with $q$.
- **Sieve.** Since $\chi$ is completely multiplicative, it suffices to
  know $\chi(p)$ for the primes $p < N$; `dlog_vec_add` computes the
  discrete logarithms of the small primes directly and obtains those of
  the larger primes cheaply from relations found by a partial extended
  Euclidean algorithm, then fills in all $k < N$ by multiplicativity. Each
  odd component contributes its part of the exponent, and the 2-part is
  added directly. This is best for $N$ much smaller than $q$.

## Use in FLINT {#use}

- `dirichlet_group_init` factors $q$ and computes the Conrey generators
  and their lifts; `dirichlet_group_dlog_precompute` builds
  `dlog_precomp_modpe_init` tables for each component.
- `dirichlet_char_log` computes the log of a number with `dlog_once`,
  `dlog_mod2e_1mod4` or the precomputed tables; `_dirichlet_char_exp`
  goes back with modular powers.
- `dirichlet_conductor_char`, `dirichlet_parity_char` and
  `dirichlet_order_char` use the log ([](#prop-conductor)); the `_ui`
  versions use the number, with modular powers, a Jacobi symbol
  ([](#prop-parity)) and the factorisation of the group exponent.
- `dirichlet_chi_vec` uses the loop over the group when $2N > \varphi(q)$
  and the sieve otherwise.
- `acb_dirichlet` builds on these functions for the numerical values,
  Gauss sums and $L$-functions.
