These functions multiply polynomials over an `mpn_mod` ring. The
dispatchers `_mpn_mod_poly_mullow` and `_mpn_mod_poly_mulmid` are the
ring's `_gr_poly_mullow` and `_gr_poly_mulmid` methods, so every generic
polynomial algorithm over the ring (`gr_poly_mul`, Newton iteration,
half-gcd, composition, ...) multiplies through them. The other functions
are the individual algorithms, useful for testing and profiling.

All of them multiply the residues as integers in $[0, n)$ and reduce
each output coefficient once, at the end. The algorithms differ in how
they compute the integer coefficients of the product: schoolbook
multiplication and Karatsuba on fixed-size limb arrays, Kronecker
substitution into one large integer product, or number-theoretic
transforms modulo small primes.

## Windows {#windows}

A middle product with window $[\mathit{nlo}, \mathit{nhi})$ computes only
the coefficients $c_i$, $\mathit{nlo} \le i < \mathit{nhi}$, of
$c = ab$, and stores $c_{\mathit{nlo}}$ at `res[0]`. Coefficients of
$a$ and $b$ that cannot contribute to the window are skipped first: terms
of index at least $\mathit{nhi}$, and, for $\mathit{nlo} > 0$, the
lowest coefficients of an operand that is longer than the number
$\mathit{len1} + \mathit{len2} - 1 - \mathit{nlo}$ of coefficients from
$\mathit{nlo}$ to the end of the product (they only reach indices below
$\mathit{nlo}$). After this, `_mpn_mod_poly_mulmid` chooses an algorithm
for the remaining lengths.

## Choice of algorithm {#choice}

Let $m = \min(\mathit{len1}, \mathit{len2})$ after the truncation.

1. If $m < 4$ or the window has at most 4 coefficients, the schoolbook
   algorithm (`_mpn_mod_poly_mulmid_classical`) is used.
2. A cutoff $c$ is read from one of five tables indexed by the bit size
   $b$ of $n$ in steps of 16 bits, for squaring, low squaring, unbalanced
   products ($\max(\mathit{len1}, \mathit{len2}) \ge 2m$), full products
   and other windows. The tables were generated by the profiling program
   `p-poly_mullow`; $c$ is between 25 and 173 and decreases as $b$ grows.
   For example, for full products of balanced operands $c$ is 91 at 80
   bits, 95 at 256 bits, 54 at 512 bits and 25 at 1024 bits.
3. If $m < c$ and $\max(\mathit{len1}, \mathit{len2}) < 4m$, the product
   is computed without intermediate reductions by
   `_mpn_mod_poly_mulmid_mpn`, which leaves the choice between the
   schoolbook algorithm and Karatsuba to `_flint_mpn_poly_mulmid` of
   `mpn_extras`, with its own tuned cutoff.
4. Otherwise the small-prime FFT (`_mpn_mod_poly_mulmid_fft_small`) is
   tried, and if it declines (`fft_small` unavailable, or $n$ larger than
   about 190 bits), Kronecker substitution
   (`_mpn_mod_poly_mulmid_KS`) is used. The tables assume that the FFT
   always beats Kronecker substitution when it can be used, which is
   nearly but not always true.

## Schoolbook and Karatsuba {#karatsuba}

The schoolbook algorithm computes each $c_i = \sum_j a_j b_{i-j}$ as an
exact sum of full products in $s$ limbs and reduces it with
`mpn_mod_set_mpn`. A sum of at most $m$ products of residues below
$n < 2^b$ is less than $m \, 2^{2b}$, so

$$
s = \max\left(\left\lceil \frac{2b + \lfloor \log_2 m \rfloor + 1}{\bits} \right\rceil,\; 2\ell - 1\right)
$$

limbs suffice, usually $2\ell$ or $2\ell + 1$. The sums are computed by
the generated fixed-size kernels of `mpn_extras` (with the dot products
inlined into the loop over the output coefficients) for $\ell \le 4$
and with `flint_mpn_mul_n` and `mpn_add_n` otherwise. The first and last
coefficients, which are single products, are computed with
`mpn_mod_mul` instead.

Karatsuba's algorithm [@KO1962] splits each operand into a low and a
high half and computes the three half-size products $a_0 b_0$,
$a_1 b_1$ and $(a_0 + a_1)(b_0 + b_1)$ recursively. Over $\Z/n\Z$ with
reduced coefficients the sums $a_0 + a_1$ would need reductions, and the
middle term needs a subtraction; FLINT instead runs the whole recursion
on the unreduced integer coefficients, which grow by about one bit per
level for the sums and are allowed a sign, and reduces only the final
coefficients. The output size bound of `_flint_mpn_poly_mulmid_slimbs`
accounts for the growth: $2b + 2 \lfloor \log_2 m \rfloor + 3$ bits (with
one extra limb when the operand coefficients have no spare high bit and
the recursion must extend them). This is why Karatsuba pays off from
short lengths: an addition of residues of $\ell$ limbs costs much less
than a multiplication.

## Kronecker substitution {#ks}

Kronecker substitution [@Kro1882; @Sch1982] evaluates both polynomials
at $x = 2^k$ with $k = 2b + \lfloor \log_2 m \rfloor + 1$, which exceeds
the bit size of every coefficient of the integer product, multiplies the
two resulting integers with `flint_mpn_mul` (which uses GMP, or
`fft_small` for large operands) and reads the coefficients off the
product $k$ bits at a time, reducing each modulo $n$. Only the windowed
coefficients are extracted. The packing wastes the padding bits but
reduces the polynomial product to a single integer product, which is
efficient for large $n$, where the small-prime FFT is not available.

## Small-prime FFT {#fft-small}

`_mpn_mod_poly_mulmid_fft_small` reduces every coefficient modulo the
50-bit primes of `fft_small`, multiplies the polynomials modulo each
prime with truncated number-theoretic transforms and reconstructs the
integer coefficients by Chinese remaindering, reducing them modulo $n$
directly from the remainder representation. It needs a product of
primes exceeding $\mathit{len2} \cdot 2^{2b + 1}$, and at most eight
primes are available, which limits $b$ to about 190 (196 bits for very
short products, 189 bits at length $10^5$); above that it returns
`GR_UNABLE`. See [](algorithms/fft/multimodular) and
[](algorithms/fft/small-prime-fft) for the transforms and the
reconstruction.

## Example {#example}

The program below computes a full product and a middle product of two
polynomials of length 3 modulo $2^{127} - 1$, the second time also with
Kronecker substitution. Coefficients are stored in arrays of
`MPN_MOD_MAX_LIMBS` limbs per slot to be independent of the word size;
`nl` is the actual number of limbs.

```c example name=mpn_mod_poly_mulmid
#include <flint/fmpz.h>
#include <flint/gr.h>
#include <flint/mpn_mod.h>

#define MAXL MPN_MOD_MAX_LIMBS

static void print_poly(nn_srcptr a, slong len, gr_ctx_t ctx)
{
    slong i, nl = MPN_MOD_CTX_NLIMBS(ctx);
    fmpz_t c;
    fmpz_init(c);
    for (i = 0; i < len; i++)
    {
        GR_MUST_SUCCEED(mpn_mod_get_fmpz(c, a + i * nl, ctx));
        fmpz_print(c);
        printf(i + 1 < len ? " " : "\n");
    }
    fmpz_clear(c);
}

int main(void)
{
    gr_ctx_t ctx;
    fmpz_t p;
    ulong f[3 * MAXL], g[3 * MAXL], h[5 * MAXL];
    slong i, nl;
    int status = GR_SUCCESS;

    fmpz_init(p);
    fmpz_ui_pow_ui(p, 2, 127);
    fmpz_sub_ui(p, p, 1);
    GR_MUST_SUCCEED(gr_ctx_init_mpn_mod(ctx, p));
    nl = MPN_MOD_CTX_NLIMBS(ctx);

    /* f = 1 + 2x + 3x^2, g = 4 + 5x - 6x^2 */
    for (i = 0; i < 3; i++)
    {
        status |= mpn_mod_set_si(f + i * nl, i + 1, ctx);
        status |= mpn_mod_set_si(g + i * nl, i == 2 ? -6 : i + 4, ctx);
    }

    /* full product: coefficients 0 to 4 */
    status |= _mpn_mod_poly_mullow(h, f, 3, g, 3, 5, ctx);
    print_poly(h, 5, ctx);

    /* middle product: coefficients 1, 2 and 3 */
    status |= _mpn_mod_poly_mulmid(h, f, 3, g, 3, 1, 4, ctx);
    print_poly(h, 3, ctx);

    /* the same middle product by Kronecker substitution */
    status |= _mpn_mod_poly_mulmid_KS(h, f, 3, g, 3, 1, 4, ctx);
    print_poly(h, 3, ctx);

    gr_ctx_clear(ctx);
    fmpz_clear(p);
    printf("status %d\n", status);
    return 0;
}
```

```output
4 13 16 3 170141183460469231731687303715884105709
13 16 3
13 16 3
status 0
```

The leading coefficient is $-18 \bmod (2^{127} - 1)$: residues are always
printed in $[0, n)$.
